# Research — Evidence Base for the Redesign

**Date:** 2026-09-24 · **Purpose:** decide what the redesigned app should do, based on scientific evidence rather than the v1 plan.

## Files

| File | What it holds |
|------|---------------|
| `evidence-brief.md` | General review: what works for adult pronunciation and articulation training in English and French. 13 topics, 145 verified findings. |
| `learner-profile-addendum.md` | The same evidence applied to this learner: French first language, English as the main target, clearer native French, phone sessions of 5–15 minutes. 29 verified findings. |
| `data/verified-findings.json` | The raw findings behind both briefs, with citations, strength labels, and checker verdicts. Also lists findings the checkers dropped. |

## How the review worked

1. One research agent per topic searched the literature and opened each source.
2. A separate checker agent re-checked every finding: does the source exist, does it support the claim, is the strength label right, is there newer contrary evidence. Checkers corrected overstated claims (96) and dropped findings they could not confirm (4).
3. A critic looked for gaps. Four gap topics got the same research-and-check treatment.
4. A writer turned the checked findings into each brief. A final checker compared the brief line by line with the findings and fixed unsupported or overstated statements.

## Limits

- Most sources were read through abstracts, author copies, or index pages, not full texts. Numbers marked "not re-checked" or "secondary" need checking before they appear in product docs.
- French evidence is thin compared with English.
- Two analyses (speechocean762 re-analysis, Lexique 3.83 minimal-pair count) were done by the review agents and are not peer reviewed.
